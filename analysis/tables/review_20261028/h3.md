# H3: rendering changes versus repeated identical requests

Exploratory post-review analysis; frozen primary results unchanged

## Method

A group is one DFA pair, model, and prompt, observed under both renderings. The first comparison uses samples 0 and 1 from each rendering. The sensitivity uses samples 0, 1, and 2. Each analysis retains only groups where every selected call parses, so its cross-rendering and within-rendering rates use the same groups. No parse failure is reclassified in the primary results.

For two calls per rendering, each group contributes four cross-rendering and two within-rendering comparisons; with three calls, these counts are nine and six. These comparisons are correlated, not independent observations. The paired difference is cross-rendering minus within-rendering disagreement. Intervals resample whole DFA pairs, retaining all their models and prompts.

Bootstrap: 10,000 resamples; seed 20261028; 95% percentile intervals.

The matched estimates do not establish additional disagreement attributable to rendering beyond repeated-call variability. This does not prove equivalence of the rates. The original 58/1,047 primary cross-rendering flips remain a valid description, but a nonzero flip rate alone does not isolate a rendering effect.

The review's conversion of 8.5% three-call disagreement to about 5.7% pairwise disagreement is a useful diagnostic: for three Boolean answers that are not all equal, exactly two of the three unordered pairs disagree. However, that overall audit denominator differs from the primary rendering denominator. The matched analysis above avoids comparing those different subsets.

## Limits and provenance

Conditional on all selected calls parsing; audit subset only. Calls were not a newly randomized rendering-versus-repeat experiment; timing and deployment drift remain possible. A CI including zero does not prove equality.

The choice of this comparison was made after review, not before inference. Model/prompt/family breakdowns are descriptive, without multiplicity-adjusted tests. The complete-case filter may select easier calls and is not evidence about unparseable decisions. No new model calls were made.
